What to observe

Try XOR, then increase the hidden-layer size. A model with too few neurons may not capture a curved or separated pattern. More layers can learn richer representations, but also increase computation.

Noise and generalization

Noise makes examples less orderly. The model may need more training, and its boundary may become less certain. A complex model can also fit noise instead of the underlying pattern.

Training time

More neurons, layers, and training steps require more calculations. This demo trains a small model locally in your browser; larger real-world networks require much more compute.

3. Six Essential Components

The components work together to transform data, measure errors, and improve predictions.

1

Layers

A layer is a group of neurons at the same stage of processing. The input layer receives features, hidden layers learn intermediate patterns, and the output layer produces the final prediction.

2

Neurons

A neuron receives values from the previous layer, calculates a weighted sum plus a bias, and applies an activation function. It acts as a basic processing unit.

3

Weights

Weights determine how strongly one neuron influences another. During training, the network adjusts these values so useful signals have more influence on the prediction.

4

Activation Functions

An activation function transforms a neuron's result and introduces nonlinearity. ReLU, sigmoid, and tanh are common examples. Without nonlinear activations, stacked layers behave like one linear transformation.

5

Loss Functions

A loss function measures the difference between predictions and the correct answers. For classification, cross-entropy is common; for regression, mean squared error is often used. Lower loss generally indicates better fit.

6

Optimization Algorithms

An optimizer updates weights and biases to reduce loss. Gradient descent uses gradients to choose updates; variants such as SGD with momentum can make learning more stable or faster.

How training works: The network makes a prediction (forward pass), calculates its loss, sends error information backward through the network (backpropagation), and uses an optimizer to update weights. This cycle repeats over many training steps.

4. Summary — Why Visualization Matters

Visualizing a neural network makes an abstract learning process easier to understand. The architecture shows how information travels through layers, while the decision boundary provides a visible result of what the model has learned.

  • Layers and neurons determine how information is represented and processed.
  • Weights and activation functions allow the network to learn useful nonlinear patterns.
  • Loss functions measure prediction errors, and optimizers guide improvements.
  • Model complexity, noise, and training settings affect both performance and training time.

Key insight: A larger network is not automatically a better network. The goal is to choose a model that captures the pattern without becoming unnecessarily complex or fitting random noise.
